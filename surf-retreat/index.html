The pitch

Most surf trips to Mexico are one of two things: a hostel with a schedule, or a villa someone flew in to rent for the week.

This is neither. I live in Puerto Escondido. The surf coach, the chef, the trainers — they're my neighbours, and they've been doing this here for years. I know which corner the colectivo stops at and which taco stand is worth the walk.

You show up. That's the whole ask.

The week

Rhythm, not a timetable. Waves don't take appointments.

Mornings — water. Five surf sessions with Coco and his team. Carrizalillo when it's your first time on a board, La Punta when you're ready for a longer ride. He picks us up and drops us back. Boards included.

One morning — into town. We take the colectivo like everyone here does and train at a local studio, alongside people who actually live in Puerto. It's twenty pesos and it's the part you'll tell people about.

Afternoons — recovery, not wellness. Guided mobility at the house, built around what paddling actually does to your shoulders. Mid-week, contrast therapy — sauna and ice bath with guided breathing. Not a spa treatment: it's how you handle the moment a wave holds you under and your body decides to panic.

Evenings — the long table. We cook breakfast together every morning and eat dinner together most nights. One night Chicama cooks for us in the house.

Two nights are loud. One is a sunset set on the roof — I DJ, we dance until the light goes. The other we head into town together, cover included. The rest of the week is early mornings and long dinners.

And two nights out of the ordinary. Swimming in the bioluminescence at Manialtepec, and a day down the coast to Mazunte.

Who this is for

This is your week if:

  • You surf sometimes — beginner or intermediate — and want to actually improve
  • You'd travel alone but would rather not eat alone
  • You want a real Mexican beach town, not a resort

Questions

How do I get there?

Fly into Puerto Escondido (PXM), connecting through Mexico City or Oaxaca. January books up — reserve your spot first, then buy the flight. I'll send you the flight guide and pick you up at the airport.

I've never surfed. Really?

Really. Carrizalillo is a protected bay and it's where everyone in this town learns.

How hard is the training?

It's recovery, not bootcamp. Mobility so you can still paddle on day five, not so you're sore for it.

What's the colectivo?

A shared pickup truck — how most of Puerto gets around. We go together, I know the stop, and if it's not your thing you can ride with me instead.

Is it safe to travel here alone as a woman?

Yes, and you won't be alone from the moment you land. On the night out we go together and come back together.

What if not enough people book?

The retreat runs with a minimum of 4. If it doesn't run, your deposit comes back in full.

Can I extend my stay?

Yes — tell me and I'll sort the extra nights.

Do I need to be fit?

No. You need to be able to swim.
